Over the past few decades, the role of the [EDM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_dance_music) DJ has changed. No longer humble spinners-of-discs, it's now common for DJs to be hybrid producer-performers, resulting in superacts like [Tiësto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ti%C3%ABsto), [David Guetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Guetta), and [Calvin Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Harris).

BT (Brian Transeau) is an example of a proto-version of that, although he doesn't really "spin discs" when he performs live. He was highly influential in late-90s trance, one of [my favorite genres](/music/paul-van-dyk/for-an-angel). _Movement In Still Life_ is a great album, but his later work is more refined. (I especially like [A Song Across Wires](https://open.spotify.com/album/1BXv3oFurqT5x2HGfg921u).)

_Never Gonna Come Back Down_ was one of my favorite electronic tracks of the late 90s, especially with Mike Doughty (of [Soul Coughing](/music/soul-coughing/soft-serve), one of my favorite bands at the time) on vocals.

Looking back now, the track offers a glimpse into the future, where an EDM producer would collaborate with an established artist. Now that EDM has become mainstream, collaborations like this can spawn pop megahits like [We Found Love](https://open.spotify.com/track/6qn9YLKt13AGvpq9jfO8py), a joint effort between Calvin Harris and Rihanna.

I also just recently learned that BT was responsive for the original soundtracks of [The Fast and the Furious](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0232500/) and [Go](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0139239/), movies that I confess to enjoying a lot when I was a teenager.
